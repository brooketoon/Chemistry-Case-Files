Case file 01

You are the commission's analyst

Cedar Bend Generating Station has powered this region for decades, and its license is running out. The State Energy Commission has to decide what happens next, and it has asked you for an independent recommendation.

Start by saying where you stand before you see any evidence. You can change your mind later. Changing your mind for a stated reason is worth more here than being right the first time.

Standards, objectives, and how you are scored HS+C.P1U3.8 · HS.P1U3.4

Arizona science standards

HS+C.P1U3.8

Engage in argument from evidence regarding the ethical, social, economic, and/or political benefits and liabilities of fission, fusion, and radioactive decay.

HS.P1U3.4

Obtain, evaluate, and communicate information about how the use of chemistry-related technologies have had positive and negative ethical, social, economic, and/or political implications.

Objective

Develop and justify an evidence-based recommendation for what should happen to Cedar Bend Generating Station by applying nuclear chemistry and weighing scientific evidence, benefits, liabilities, and broader implications.

Along the way you will compare fission, fusion, and radioactive decay; identify a benefit and a liability of each; separate evidence from opinion; and name the cost you accept, the strongest case against you, and what would change your mind.

How your brief is scored

Five things, four levels each. You can see this before you write, and it is printed on your brief.

Which pathway you recommend is not scored. Two students can reach opposite recommendations and both earn a 4.

Timing

Roughly 70 minutes of work: 5 to set a starting position, 20 on the nuclear science file, 15 on one evidence lens, 20 building the argument, and 10 with the commission. Most classes will need two periods.

Stop wherever the period ends. Work saves on the device automatically and the same browser picks up where it left off. The cleanest break is after the science file, since the evidence lens and the argument belong together.

How this is assessed

Your advisory brief at the end is the assessment. It is scored on the quality of your evidence and reasoning, not on which pathway you recommend. There is no predetermined correct position on Cedar Bend.

Evidence file · SCI-01

Three nuclear processes, side by side

The commission's decision involves three different nuclear processes. They are often confused with each other, and they carry very different benefits and liabilities. Compare them before you take a position.

Before you start

Most people use these three words as if they mean the same thing. They don't. One is happening at Cedar Bend right now, one cannot be switched off, and one has never powered a home.

Which is which?

Benefit Something good the process does. Example: it makes electricity without burning fuel.
Liability A cost, a risk, or a problem the process creates. Example: it leaves waste that stays dangerous for a long time.
Neither Some statements are false, or are true but are not about the process itself. Example: how many people a plant employs.

See an example on a different topic

Topic: Should our school start at 9:00 instead of 7:30? This writer recommends the later start.

Weak

“Someone who disagrees would say they like starting early. But they are wrong, because teens need sleep.”

Nobody who opposes the change would describe their reason this way. It is waved away, not answered.

Strong

“Someone who disagrees would say a later start pushes everything else later. Practices and jobs would end after dark, and students who pick up younger siblings at 3:00 could no longer do it. They are right that this cost falls hardest on families with the least flexibility, and a later start does not fix that.”

It gives the other side's real reasons, says who is affected, and admits what they are right about.
